How to Check Website Visitors

You can see exact visitor numbers only for a website you own. Google Analytics, Search Console and Cloudflare Analytics are free tools that show you who visits your site, which pages they read, and where they come from. This guide shows you, step by step, how to check your own website visitors.

Method 1 — Google Analytics 4 (GA4)

Google Analytics 4, or GA4, is the free tool Google gives you to measure your own website. It shows you how many people visit, which pages they read, and where they come from.

  1. Open analytics.google.com and sign in with the Google account that owns the website.
  2. Open your property — the website you want to check.
  3. Go to Reports, then Realtime, to see how many people are on your site right now.
  4. Go to Reports, then Engagement, then “Pages and screens”, to see which pages get the most views.
  5. Go to Reports, then Demographic overview, to see which countries your visitors come from.

Active users, sessions and views — what they mean

  • Active users — the number of different people who visited in the period you selected.
  • Sessions — visits, including repeat visits by the same person, so sessions are usually higher than active users.
  • Views — the number of times pages were opened, including when one person opens several pages.

At the top of every report you can change the date range, for example the last 7 days or the last 28 days. Always compare the same date range when you look at two reports, or the numbers will not match.

Method 2 — Google Search Console

Google Search Console is free and shows how your site performs in Google search. It does not need a tracking code, but you must verify that you own the site.

  1. Open search.google.com/search-console and open your property.
  2. Click Performance on the left.
  3. You will see total clicks (people who clicked your site in Google search), total impressions (how often your site appeared in search results) and average position (where your pages rank on average).
  4. Use URL Inspection at the top to check whether a single page is indexed by Google.

Search Console shows only Google search traffic, not all visitors. A person who types your address directly or clicks a link on social media will not appear here, so its numbers are always lower than Google Analytics.

Method 3 — Cloudflare Web Analytics

If your site is hosted on Cloudflare Pages, like this site, Cloudflare Web Analytics shows your visits and page views with no code to install. It also shows Core Web Vitals and page load time, so you can see how fast your site feels for visitors.

It counts visits, not people, and it does not track users across sites. It is the quickest way to check a small site, but for full detail — countries, pages and traffic sources — Google Analytics is better.

Can I check someone else’s website visitors?

No. There is no public number for another website’s exact visitors. Visitor data is private and belongs to the site owner, so no tool can show you the real total for a site you do not own.

Tools like SimilarWeb show estimates based on their own data, not the site’s real total. Treat any number you see for someone else’s site as a rough estimate, never as an exact figure — and be careful with any tool that claims otherwise.

Use your numbers with GeoFix tools

Once you know your numbers, paste them into our free tools to understand them better. Use the Geo Traffic Analyzer to see what share of your visitors comes from your target country, and the Website Value Calculator to estimate what your site could be worth from its traffic.

Frequently Asked Questions

Is it free to check my website visitors?

Yes. Google Analytics 4, Google Search Console and Cloudflare Web Analytics are all free. You only need a Google account and access to the website.

Do I need to install anything?

For Google Analytics you add a small tracking code or a Google tag to your site. Search Console only needs you to verify that you own the site, and Cloudflare Analytics needs no code at all on Cloudflare Pages.

Why does GA4 show different numbers from Search Console?

They measure different things. GA4 counts all visitors from every source, while Search Console counts only clicks from Google search. The two reports also use different date ranges by default, so check that both cover the same days before comparing them.

How long until a new site shows data?

Google Analytics starts collecting data as soon as the tag is installed, but you need at least a day of visits before the reports are useful. Search Console can take a few days to show clicks, and a new page can take days to two weeks to appear in Google search at all.

Can I see who exactly visited my site?

No. Analytics tools show totals, countries, devices and pages, but never the names, email addresses or personal details of individual visitors.
